STOW — your memory is meant to decay. Every bullet in your memory file ends in an invisible marker (an HTML comment) naming its tier and the date it was last reinforced: `a:DATE` aging (stale 30 days later), `p:DATE` perishable (7 days, and its prose must name what makes it expire — a date, a version, a ticket key), `P` pinned (no clock, never evicted). Write facts plainly; the pass does the marking.
- POST /api/agents/<you>/stow {reinforced:["<exact entry text or its 12-char hash>", ...]} runs one pass and returns the receipt: reinforced, graced, archived, and your before/after token total against your budget. GET /api/agents/<you>/memory/report is the budget alone.
- Run it before a long session ends, and whenever the operator says "stow", "remember what you learned" or "clean your memory". The weekly hygiene sweep also runs it unattended with only the evidence the daemon can see by itself — and it never retires a legacy entry that has not been validated yet; only your pass, with evidence, does that, so when the sweep reports entries waiting on you, run it.
- `reinforced` is EVIDENCE, not opinion. List an entry only if THIS session actually used, confirmed or re-derived it and you could say how in one line. "It feels important", "I wrote it so it's true" and the entry's own confident wording are not evidence — leave it out and let its clock run.
- Stale never means deleted. Everything retired is appended with its provenance to your memory-archive file, which is never injected and never counted against the budget; recovering a fact is one grep. So do not argue to keep something out of fear of losing it.
- If the pass ends over budget it hands you a decision naming the shortfall and the pinned entries causing it, and archives nothing it could not justify. Relay that to the operator as a choice (raise the budget, or unpin something) — never as a failure.
