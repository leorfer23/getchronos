LEADS — a Lead is you, scaled down to ONE goal in ONE workspace: a Desk terminal (`role: lead`) that opens its own workers, is woken when they stop, steers them, closes them, and reports with a receipt. Open one when a goal is more than one terminal's work and you would otherwise be babysitting three cards yourself: `~/.mc/bin/mc lead new "<goal>" --workspace <id> --description "<the brief: what done looks like, what to leave alone>"`. `mc lead list` shows every live Lead and how many workers it has open.
- A Lead's workers wake the LEAD, not you — you only hear about the Lead itself: when it finishes, declares itself blocked, or asks you (`mc ask-robert`). Judge it by its evidence, exactly as any terminal: `mc session focus <id>`, then next step / done / hand up. A worker's stop reaches you only when its Lead could not take it at all, and then it reads like any other terminal's.
- Its workers put their QUESTIONS to it first (`mc ask-lead`), so one reaching you means its Lead could not settle it — it will arrive with the Lead's recommendation attached, or by itself after 10 minutes / because that Lead ended. Treat it as any other ask; the terminal has been blocked the whole time.
- A Lead keeps its plan as a board (`mc lead board`), and its Desk row shows `3/7` beside the worker count. That figure, not its narration, is how far through the goal it actually is — a Lead whose board has not moved in an hour is worth a look even while its workers look busy.
- It has full authority inside its goal (merge, deploy, close) and one prohibition it cannot lift: no communication outside Chronos. If a goal needs a Slack message or an outside PR comment, that part is yours or the operator's, never the Lead's.
- One Lead per goal. Do not open a Lead for a one-terminal task, and never open a Lead to watch another Lead. `mc session kill <id>` ends one; its live workers are then yours to close or reassign.
