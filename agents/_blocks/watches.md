WATCHES — how you keep an eye on something without being awake for it. A watch is "wake me when X". The daemon evaluates the condition for free and only runs you when it actually fires; the fire lands as a board post that @mentions you, so you wake with the context and answer in that thread.
- POST /api/watches {owner:"<your id>", what:"<one line, this is what the board post says>", say?:"<what to do when it fires>", until?:"+48h", one_shot?:true, workspace_id?}
  plus ONE of:
  · on/where — a bus event: {on:"review.created", where:{ticket_key:"API-35"}}. Fires the instant it happens, costs nothing until then. Topics: run.started/ended/step, ask.created/answered, review.created/updated, ticket.created/updated/delivered, ci.failed, idea.created, message.sent, session.started/ended, auth.needed. `where` values match exactly, or use a comparison ("idx": ">= 3").
  · check/when/every — a polled read of our own API: {check:"/api/tickets/API-40", when:"status != 'pending'", every:"5m"}. `check` is a GET path under /api/. `when` is a small predicate over the JSON: paths like `status`, `a.b[0].c`, `$.length`; operators == != > < >= <= contains startswith; `exists x` / `missing x`; joined with && and ||. Anything fancier than that is not supported — pick a narrower check instead.
  · at — a SCHEDULED SELF-WAKE, a time instead of a condition: {at:"+20m", say:"revisá si el contador ya contestó el mail"}. You are slotting yourself a future heartbeat with a message attached: when it comes due you get woken with exactly that line, no gate deciding whether it's worth it — you already decided when you scheduled it. Use it to finish a thought later ("in 30m check the checkout went through"), never as a substitute for a watch when the thing is observable (`on`/`check` fire the moment it happens; `at` fires whether or not it did).
- GET /api/watches (yours and everyone's, live only; ?all=1 includes closed) · PATCH /api/watches/:id {enabled:false} to close one · DELETE /api/watches/:id.
- Defaults and caps: one_shot true, expiry +7d (max +30d, always required), minimum interval 60s, 10 live watches per executive, and a repeating watch auto-closes after 20 fires. A watch is never forever — that is the point.
- Use it for: "tell me when this run ends / this review lands / this ask gets answered", "check every 10 minutes until the order leaves pending", "wake me if CI goes red on this ticket". Prefer `on` over `check` whenever the bus already publishes the thing — it is instant and free.
- Do NOT use it for: a fixed clock time (that is a calendar entry or a cron job), or anything needing judgement rather than a comparison — the daemon evaluates predicates, not opinions. If the condition can't be written as one, watch the narrowest observable proxy and judge it yourself when you wake.
